Abstract

A robot that lifts an egg, a ripe peach, and a cold can covered in condensation must hold each of them differently, even when it recognizes all three correctly. Vision-language-action (VLA) models decide how to act from appearance and language, and their actions barely change when an object's mass, friction, fragility, or deformability changes. To locate where this breaks, we give the same policy ground-truth physical properties in three ways. Setting the gripper command directly from these properties removes [X]% of failures, while stating them as text leaves actions nearly unchanged. The knowledge is useful, but the language channel does not carry it to the action. Building on this observation, we introduce PhysCue, which estimates physical property intervals from visual cues such as material, volume, and surface appearance, and uses them to bound the gripper command and motion speed of a frozen VLA. Motor current refines the interval after contact. On RoboCasa tasks spanning rigid, fragile, and deformable objects, PhysCue reduces failures by [Y]% over property-aware baselines, and correcting only the property responsible for a failure fixes that failure without affecting others.
